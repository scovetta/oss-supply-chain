# Book 3: Governing the Software Supply Chain

Technical controls fail without people, money, and rules to support them. This book covers the organizational, legal, and economic side of supply chain security: who is responsible, who pays, and what governments now require.

It begins with people: training developers, building security champion programs, and supporting the open source maintainers whose work everyone depends on. It then covers vendor risk management and the regulatory landscape, including U.S. federal requirements, the EU Cyber Resilience Act (whose reporting obligations took effect in September 2026), sector-specific rules, and the compliance frameworks that translate them into controls. Later chapters examine the economics of open source security, the industry initiatives that fund and coordinate it, the geopolitics of a global commons, and lessons from industries that have managed physical supply chains for decades. The book closes with where the field is heading, including how AI-driven vulnerability discovery is changing the balance between finding bugs and fixing them.
