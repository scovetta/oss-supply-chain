# Book 1: Understanding the Software Supply Chain

Almost nobody writes most of the software they ship. A typical application is assembled from hundreds or thousands of open source packages, pulled from public registries, built on shared CI services, and delivered through update channels the developer never sees. Each of those steps is a place where someone else's mistake, or someone else's attack, becomes your problem.

This book explains how that system works and how it fails. The first part covers how software is built today: the ecosystems, package managers, and dependency relationships that most teams rely on without examining, and the volunteers who maintain much of it. The second part works through the attacks in detail, from typosquatting and dependency confusion to compromised build systems, hijacked maintainer accounts, multi-year social engineering campaigns like the XZ Utils backdoor, and the new attack surfaces created by AI coding tools. Each pattern is grounded in real incidents, with what happened, who was affected, and what defenders learned.
